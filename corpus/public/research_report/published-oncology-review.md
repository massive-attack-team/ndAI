# Review: Dose-Dependent Hepatotoxicity in Oncology Candidates

Across published small-molecule oncology programmes, dose-limiting
hepatotoxicity is a recurring finding in preclinical development, typically
identified through ALT and AST elevation in high-dose cohorts relative to
vehicle control.

Intermittent dosing schedules are an established mitigation strategy in the
published literature when a compound shows a narrow therapeutic window -
several published programmes report preserving efficacy while reducing
cumulative exposure using on/off dosing schedules rather than continuous
administration.

Tumour volume reduction relative to vehicle control remains the standard
efficacy readout at the study endpoint, most commonly reported at day 21 in
xenograft models, consistent with general practice in the field.
