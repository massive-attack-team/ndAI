# Published Methods: High-Throughput Screening Assays

Cell-based screening assays are normalised against on-plate controls so that
plate-to-plate variation does not propagate into hit calling. Percent inhibition
is computed relative to the high and low control wells on the same plate.

The Z-factor is the standard measure of assay quality. Assays with a Z-factor
below 0.5 are generally considered unsuitable for screening, and most groups
quarantine or repeat those plates rather than analysing them.

Dose-response relationships are usually fitted with a four-parameter logistic
model, which yields an IC50 together with a Hill slope. Reporting the fit
parameters alongside the IC50 is now expected in most journals.

Tumour volume reduction relative to vehicle control is the common efficacy
endpoint in xenograft studies. Hepatotoxicity is frequently dose limiting for
small molecule oncology candidates, and ALT elevation beyond three times the
upper limit of normal is a widely used signal of liver injury.
